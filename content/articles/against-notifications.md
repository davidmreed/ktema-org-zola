title="Against Notifications"
draft=true
+++

Notifications in software often represent a lack of trust.

Here's what I mean. When you're told "So-and-so needs to get an email when this happens," or "Log the statistics to Slack every hour," or ... there is an underlying problem with trust. To get to that problem, you need to ask at least two "whys". 

The answer to the first "why" is usually "So they can stay involved/monitor the process," which means very little. The subsequent "whys" are where it gets interesting, because they're almost always about trust and control.

Why would a leader want to observe a process in action? Overt or not, that need for visibility reflects a lack of trust in the staff running the process or the system mediating it. What decision or intervention will that data inform? 

Why would a stakeholder need to get an email when someone else takes an action? 

Why would a team need to get Slack messages about their own services in normal operation? They don't trust their monitoring and alerting.

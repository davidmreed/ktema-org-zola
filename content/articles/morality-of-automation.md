I occasionally reflect on building automation and wonder whether it's a moral good.

In a previous role, I built a tool that would massively improve process efficiency across a large, mostly-manual department. I had hints, but never a clear statement, that the automation offered by this tool would likely lead to layoffs in that department. I also talked to users in that department, and received generally positive reviews for the experience I was creating.

I built the tool. I did not stay in that role, and genuinely don't know what the aftermath was.

I always want to focus on the positive side of automation. I genuinely believe that using automation to free humans from repetitive, un-creative work is a good thing. It gives people time to create value for themselves, for others, and for their organizations. Great companies recognize this, and take full advantage of the time their newly-freed employees have to do creative work, human-facing work, or simply to write more code.

But there is another side, where automation is used as a tool by corporations _solely_ to slash their costs - not by empowering people, but by removing people. I suspect the situation where this happens often resembles the one I saw earlier in my career. The staff whose work is being automated are experts, but they're commoditized experts. The work they're doing is skilled, but not creative and not innovative.

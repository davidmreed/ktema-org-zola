I believe that the "in-office/remote" dichotomy is a false one. The true dichotomy is whether you're successfully distributed or not.

Distributed organizations are agile and adaptable. They succeed with any combination of office-based, remote, and flexible teams. The async-friendly, systems-first, artifact-oriented workflows they use allow teams to maintain steady output and a deep knowledge base despite arbitrary disruptions. Contributors bring their best no matter where they are.

Non-distributed organizations can succeed with a lower cost of entry and less systems rigor - but they're inflexible. When their assumptions break, their workflows, and their productivity, break too. And they leave on the table the benefits they could be getting by including team members for whom their inflexible structure is a deal-breaker.

Non-distributed organizations rely on implicit solutions to collaboration problems that distributed organizations must solve explicitly. That's cheap, yes - but it's fragile, and leaves the organization without insights into why their teams are successful.

If you're distributed, who cares where people work?
